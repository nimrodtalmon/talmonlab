Steering a Living Allocator
with Scarce Attention

An AI splits a fund; a community keeps it on course

Nimrod Talmon · Ben-Gurion University · draft, November 18, 2026

Motivation

An AI splits a stream of funding

Money flows all the time; when the split goes wrong, someone should notice.

Motivation

A real case: Deep Funding, round GG24

An AI splits $350k among 98 open-source repos; human jurors compare pairs.

scarce

About 50 jurors gave about 500 judgments; motivation was the main constraint.

noisy

Jurors disagreed on which project ranks higher 40% of the time.

fragile

One answer rating a project 999× another moved about a quarter of the funding.

wanted

The trial’s analysis asks for continuous funding with occasional juror input.

Model

Continuous funding

Projects \(1, \dots, m\); time \(t \ge 0\).

Funding flows at rate 1; at time \(t\), project \(j\) gets the share \(\mathrm{share}_t(j)\) (shares sum to 1).

Engine: sets the split \(\mathrm{share}_t\) at every moment.

Gap of projects \(a, b\): \(\mathrm{gap}_{ab} = \log \tfrac{\mathrm{share}(a)}{\mathrm{share}(b)}\)

First, a benevolent engine: it follows the world, but errs persistently on some projects.

Model

Members notice problems

Members \(1, \dots, n\); view \(\mathrm{view}_i\): the split \(i\) considers right. Some maintain a project.

Noticing: member \(i\) looks at project \(j\) at random times, at rate \(\mathrm{notice}_{ij}(t)\); it may grow with how wrong \(j\) is, and for how long.

Flag: on noticing, \(i\) may mark \(j\) as too low or too high.

The chance that a problem is still unnoticed falls with its age.

Illustration: one problem, many members.

Model

Members answer questions

Judged gap: \(d_{i,ab} = \log \tfrac{\mathrm{view}_i(a)}{\mathrm{view}_i(b)} + \text{noise}\)

Question to \(i\) on \(a, b\): shows their shares; the answer is \(\operatorname{sign}(d_{i,ab} - \mathrm{gap}_{ab})\)

Attention: answered questions per unit of time.

Flags are nearly free; answers cost attention.

Model

A constitution turns answers into moves

Constitution: questions on \(a, b\) arrive at rate \(\mathrm{rate}_{ab}\) (may depend on flags); whom to ask; the update.

\(d_{ab}\): the judged gap of the member asked about \(a, b\).

Nudge: answer \(s\) multiplies \(\tfrac{\mathrm{share}(a)}{\mathrm{share}(b)}\) by \(e^{s \cdot \mathrm{step}}\)

Multiplier (the round’s): typed ratio \(x\); \(\mathrm{gap}_{ab}\) moves by \(\mathrm{lr}\cdot(\log x - \mathrm{gap}_{ab})\)

A nudge uses an answer’s direction, a multiplier its size. \(\mathrm{step}=0.1\), \(\mathrm{lr}=0.3\).

Model

Objective

Target \(\mathrm{target}_t\): for every pair \(a, b\),

\(\log \tfrac{\mathrm{target}_t(a)}{\mathrm{target}_t(b)} = \operatorname{median}_i\, d_{i,ab}\)

Misallocated funding up to time \(T\):

\(\int_0^T \tfrac12 \textstyle\sum_j |\mathrm{share}_t(j) - \mathrm{target}_t(j)|\, dt\)

Goal: little misallocated funding for little attention.

A problem costs its size times how long it lasts.

One project; the shaded area is funding paid to the wrong place.

Observation

Nudges minimize a loss at the median

In the small-step limit, nudges follow the gradient flow of

\(\mathrm{loss}(\mathrm{share}) = \tfrac{\mathrm{step}}{2} \sum_{a \lt b} \mathrm{rate}_{ab}\, \mathbb{E}\,|d_{ab} - \mathrm{gap}_{ab}|\)

The loss is convex; its minimizers are the resting points.

The derivative of \(\mathbb{E}|d - \mathrm{gap}|\) in \(\mathrm{gap}\) is minus the expected answer.

Judged gaps from a skewed distribution.

Proposition 1

Bounded influence

One answer changes any log-ratio of two shares by at most \(\mathrm{step}\).

If a member answers at most \(k\) questions a day, then a factor of \(e^{k \cdot \mathrm{step}}\) bounds how much they change any ratio of two shares in a day.

Immediate from the update. A multiplier answer moves a log-ratio by \(\mathrm{lr}\cdot(\log x - \mathrm{gap})\), unbounded as the cap on \(x\) grows.

\(k = 10\), \(\mathrm{step} = 0.1\).

Proposition 2

The agenda is not the verdict

If \(\operatorname{median}\, d_{ab} = \log \tfrac{\mathrm{target}(a)}{\mathrm{target}(b)}\) for every pair, then \(\mathrm{target}\) minimizes the loss for all rates.

If also the asked pairs connect all projects and each \(d_{ab}\) has positive density at its median, then it is the unique minimizer.

Each term is smallest at its median, and \(\mathrm{target}\) attains all of them.

Simulation, inconsistent medians: 1 on (1,2) and (2,3), 0 on (1,3). The gap ends at -0.04 or 2.01.

Proposition 3

Maintainers set the overlooked projects

If maintainers give a fraction \(\mathrm{frac}_j\) of the answers about \(j\), with judged gaps shifted up by \(\mathrm{bias} > 0\), then the resting gap of \(j\) grows with \(\mathrm{frac}_j\), toward \(\mathrm{bias}\) above consensus.

The resting gap is a median of a mixture. Recusal sets \(\mathrm{frac}_j = 0\); without it, \(\mathrm{frac}_j \approx 1\) for projects nobody else looks at.

\(\mathrm{bias} = 1\); judged gaps normal, standard deviation 0.71.

Proposition 4

Exaggeration moves multipliers, not nudges

If every member types multipliers whose logarithm is inflated by \(\mathrm{infl} > 1\), then the multiplier rule rests at \(\mathrm{infl}\) times the mean gaps.

Nudges rest at the median gaps, whatever members would have typed.

Multipliers rest where the expected residual vanishes; nudges where the expected answer vanishes, which depends only on the judged gaps.

Nine judged gaps, one extreme; \(\mathrm{infl} = 2.7\), the simulation’s value.

Policy

Flag, sample, nudge, escalate

Interested attention is safe as an agenda and harmful as a verdict (Propositions 2 and 3).

Flaganyone, any time; signed, standing; opposite flags cancel; own project counts ½
→
Sample½ of questions by net flags, ½ by share, each with a random partner
→
Nudgea random member who maintains neither project; one fixed step; ≤ 10 a day
→
Escalatenet flags at a threshold: a panel answers the next questions

Each rule is a parameter of the constitution. Step and agenda change the speed; recusal and format change where the split rests.

Simulation

Five answers a day halve the engine’s error

With flag and sample, 9.7% of the funding is misallocated at 5 answered questions a day and 8.1% at 10; with the engine alone, 19.8%.

Simulation: 98 repos of the round; engine off by about 4.5× on 10% of them; 150 members, 50 maintainers; 12 runs of 180 days.

Simulation

Ask uninvolved members for a direction

At about 12 answers a day, random members with nudges leave 8.0% misallocated (7.5% with flags); an open forum, 15.2%, with 8.0% captured.

Simulation, same world. Recusal removes capture; multipliers do not improve on the engine.

Simulation

If judgments are inflated, humans stop helping

If judgments themselves are inflated by 2.7, then every human channel leaves between 18.7% and 24.2% misallocated, against 19.8% for the engine alone.

Simulation, same world; the inflation is in what members believe, not in how they type.
